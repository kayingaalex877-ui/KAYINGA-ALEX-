Shared folder and design conventions

Keep the project organized using the current shared structure:

  • PROJECT/
    • index.html, about.html, gallery.html, services.html, contact.html
    • style.css
    • images — descriptive, lowercase, hyphen-separated image filenames
    • videos/ — descriptive, lowercase, hyphen-separated media filenames

File naming: use lowercase names, hyphens between words, and the correct file extension (for example, berry-smoothie.jpg).

Colour and type: use forest green (#176b45) for navigation and headings, soft blush (#fff1f5) for the page background, and Georgia serif for body text. Keep text readable and reuse the shared stylesheet.

Session 3: Build, coordinate, and test

Use the assigned roles and shared conventions above as the team builds the pages. The current site includes the agreed home, about, gallery, services, and contact pages, with shared navigation, a common stylesheet, and the project media folders.

Keep the build consistent

  • Use the same page names, shared navigation labels, and active-page indicator.
  • Link each page to style.css and follow the agreed green, blush, and Georgia theme.
  • Keep images and video in the shared folders, with descriptive alt text and accessible controls.
  • Tell the team before changing shared styles, filenames, or page structure.

Check in regularly

  • At each team check-in, share completed work, the next task, and any blockers.
  • Review pages together for consistent navigation, spacing, headings, and links.
  • Have another teammate review each page before the final presentation.

Test at different sizes

  • Small phone: about 375 px wide
  • Tablet: about 768 px wide
  • Desktop: about 1280 px wide
  • At each size, check for clipped content, sideways page scrolling, and usable navigation.

Test before sharing

  • Open every page and follow each navigation link.
  • Check that images load, and video and audio controls are usable.
  • Try the contact form controls and check labels, required fields, and keyboard focus.
  • Record issues, assign an owner, fix them, and retest the affected page.

Team test record: add the tester's name, page, screen width, issue found, and retest result at each check-in.

Final review, presentation, and peer feedback

Team run-through

  • Open Home and follow every navigation item, product link, and page link; check the logo and back-to-top link.
  • Visit all five pages and confirm images load, have useful alternative text, and media controls appear.
  • Try the contact form empty, then with valid details; confirm required fields and email validation.
  • Check the phone link and external healthy-diet and map links.
  • Repeat the key checks at phone, tablet, and desktop widths; log issues, fix them, and retest.

Form note: the current contact form is a front-end demonstration; it has no configured message-submission service.

Presentation rehearsal — 5 minutes

  1. 0:00–0:30: introduce the team and project goal.
  2. 0:30–1:15: explain the audience, site map, and design choices.
  3. 1:15–3:30: demonstrate the key pages, navigation, media, and contact form.
  4. 3:30–4:30: show responsive behavior and describe testing and improvements.
  5. 4:30–5:00: summarize the result and invite questions.

Practice once with a timer; adjust to the instructor's required duration and let each teammate speak.

Peer feedback checklist

Ask another team to review the site and mark each item Pass, Needs work, or N/A:

  • Navigation and links are clear and work.
  • Pages are readable, consistent, and easy to use.
  • Layout works on phone, tablet, and desktop.
  • Images and media load and have useful labels or controls.
  • Form labels and required-field feedback are clear.
  • Content is complete, accurate, and easy to understand.

Feedback record: note one strength, one priority improvement, and the page or element affected.

Review record: note the rehearsal time, peer team's one strength and one improvement, and the action owner.

Presentation day and team reflection

Live presentation and demo

  • Introduce the team, project goal, and intended audience.
  • Walk through the site from Home; demonstrate navigation and key pages.
  • Show the responsive layout and the site's images and media.
  • Explain the contact form honestly: it checks required fields, but has no message-submission service.
  • Share one testing improvement, then invite questions.

Responding to questions

  • Listen to the full question and pause before answering.
  • Answer directly and point to the relevant page or design decision.
  • If unsure, say so and explain how the team would check or improve it.
  • Thank the questioner and invite another question.

Self- and peer-assessment prompts

  • What did I contribute, and which responsibility did I complete?

  • What is one strength I showed and one skill I can improve?

A little more about fresh juice

Fresh juice begins with good fruit. Choosing ripe fruit helps bring out its natural flavor and makes each drink more enjoyable. Oranges, mangoes, pineapples, and berries each bring a different color and taste to the glass.

Preparing fruit carefully is an important part of making a refreshing drink. Fruit should be washed before it is cut, blended, or pressed. Clean preparation helps keep the fruit's natural taste at the center of every recipe.

Different fruits can be enjoyed on their own or combined to create new flavors. A bright citrus juice can be balanced with sweeter fruit, while a smoothie can pair berries with other ingredients for a thicker drink.

Serving size is another way to enjoy juice thoughtfully. A glass can be part of a varied diet alongside whole fruits, vegetables, and water. The best choice is one that fits the moment and your personal preference.

At Shanitah Juice Company, we make drinks for everyday refreshment and special occasions. Whether you prefer a simple orange juice or a mixed fruit smoothie, we aim to make each serving with care.

We keep exploring fruit combinations and listening to what customers enjoy. Visit our gallery for a look at some of our drinks, or contact us with a question or flavor suggestion.
